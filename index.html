Team-based, not solo

Every other race hands you a bib and wishes you luck alone. This one is scored by community. Your club's colors, your club's name on the leaderboard, everyone's finish counts toward the same total.

02

Built like a festival

Think Spartan meets Rock 'n' Roll Marathon meets an actual festival lineup. Real production, real stages, real reasons to stick around after you cross the line instead of grabbing a banana and leaving.

03

City by city

Los Angeles goes first. Every city already has its own run club scene, its own regulars, its own inside jokes. Run Club Fest just gives them a reason to race each other once a year.

How race day actually works

  1. 1

    Your club claims a wave

    Show up under your run club's name. Ten people or forty, whoever runs that day counts toward your team.

  2. 2

    Race the course together

    Not a marathon, and not a 5K either. A course built around mixed distances and relay-style legs, so a full club roster actually matters.

  3. 3

    Points stack for the whole crew

    Placement and participation both score. The fastest team wins the day, but every runner who finishes adds to the total.

  4. 4

    Then it's a party

    The finish line opens into recovery grounds: saunas, food, drinks, a DJ. Nobody's driving home right after this one.

After the finish line

The recovery grounds are the whole second half of the day.

Picture the reset-style wellness pop-ups already happening around LA, portable saunas, cold plunge, stretch stations, dropped right into a race festival. Add food and drink vendors, a real sound system, and a DJ set that turns a parking lot into something worth staying for. Race in the morning, recover and celebrate through the afternoon.
